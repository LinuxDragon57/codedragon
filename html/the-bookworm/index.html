The Bookworm

by E. L. Jackson

Sit down.
Let me tell you the stories
Of a creature so elusive,
That some believe it a cryptid.
But while it is true
That they are going extinct;
Bookworms can be found…
If you know where to look.

Soaring on the wings of dragons and griffons,
Wandering through forest glades of folk and fae,
Venturing into the murky depths of sirens and merfolk,
Or traipsing through the mountains with giants and dwarves;
These creatures are the most adventurous species on Earth.

Or maybe they are otherworldly,
Having journeyed through space and time,
Leaping from star to star
Aboard marvelous contraptions of science.
Perhaps, instead, they are divine;
For some claim to commune with deities.
Yet, others insist they are mere soldiers
Within the armies of God: superhuman
Warriors charged with protecting souls
And hunting angels of darkness.

Enthralled by magic
Energized with the supernatural
Ghost whisperers, soothsayers
Yes, bookworms see all, hear all.
For within the intense gaze of this beastie,
Lurks the clockwork of the universe:
Galaxies spinning their heavenly dance
The rotating planets, each with their own time
And the blazing lives of stars
Winking in and out of existence.
Indeed, bookworms witness a thousand futures,
Hearken to the echoes of the past,
And live for the thrilling climaxes within each day.

Towering shelves of mahogany
Musky notes of vanilla and earth
The bookworm's true domain
Among its vast, colorful collection
Of paper leaves and ink
Bound by chemicals and glue
Between two covers.
These magnificent tomes
Fuel our hallucinations,
Tolerable by the sacrifices of dead trees.
Devouring one after another,
Books are the only true magic.
